# TakeoffCompass Preconstruction Reference: OSHA-Compliant SDS Guide

This safety reference documents the Hazard Communication Standard (HCS) requirements under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1200, detailing the mandatory 16-section Safety Data Sheet (SDS) structure and safety profiles for core materials included in the takeoff calculator.

---

## 1. The 16-Section SDS Standard Format

Under OSHA guidelines aligned with the Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals (GHS), all Safety Data Sheets must contain the following 16 sections in this precise order:

| Section | Title | Primary Required Contents |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Section 1** | **Identification** | Product identifier, manufacturer contact details, emergency phone number, and recommended uses/restrictions. |
| **Section 2** | **Hazard(s) Identification** | GHS classification, hazard statements, precautionary statements, pictograms, and signal words (*Danger* or *Warning*). |
| **Section 3** | **Composition/Information on Ingredients** | Chemical name, CAS number, synonyms, impurities, and concentrations of all hazardous ingredients. |
| **Section 4** | **First-Aid Measures** | Symptoms, effects (acute and delayed), and necessary first-aid treatments by exposure route (eye, skin, inhalation, ingestion). |
| **Section 5** | **Fire-Fighting Measures** | Extinguishing media, specific hazards arising from chemical fire, and protective equipment for firefighters. |
| **Section 6** | **Accidental Release Measures** | Personal precautions, protective equipment, emergency procedures, containment, and cleanup methods. |
| **Section 7** | **Handling and Storage** | Safe handling practices, ventilation requirements, and conditions for safe storage (incompatibilities). |
| **Section 8** | **Exposure Controls/Personal Protection** | OSHA Permissible Exposure Limits (PELs), ACGIH TLVs, engineering controls, and Personal Protective Equipment (PPE). |
| **Section 9** | **Physical and Chemical Properties** | Appearance, odor, pH, boiling/melting points, flash point, vapor pressure, solubility, and viscosity. |
| **Section 10** | **Stability and Reactivity** | Chemical stability, reactivity, possibility of hazardous reactions, incompatible materials, and decomposition products. |
| **Section 11** | **Toxicological Information** | Routes of exposure, symptoms, immediate/delayed/chronic effects, and numerical measures of toxicity (e.g., LD50). |
| **Section 12** | **Ecological Information\*** | Ecotoxicity, persistence/degradability, bioaccumulation, and soil mobility. |
| **Section 13** | **Disposal Considerations\*** | Safe handling of waste, container disposal, and regional/national recycling or disposal regulations. |
| **Section 14** | **Transport Information\*** | UN number, shipping name, hazard class, packing group, and environmental hazards. |
| **Section 15** | **Regulatory Information\*** | Safety, health, and environmental regulations specific for the product (SARA, TSCA, state right-to-know regulations). |
| **Section 16** | **Other Information** | Date of preparation, last revision date, and key explanations. |

*\*Note: OSHA enforces Sections 1 through 10, and Section 16. Sections 12 through 15 are optional under OSHA jurisdiction but mandatory for full international GHS compliance (governed by EPA, DOT, and international regulations).*

## 3. Material safety Profiles (Takeoff Assemblies)

Below are the safety profiles and exposure controls for materials scheduled in the preconstruction calculator:

### A. Gypsum Board (Drywall Panels)
*   **Hazards (Section 2)**: Eye and respiratory tract irritant. Inhalation of dust generated during cutting may contain **crystalline silica** (Group 1 Carcinogen).
*   **Exposure Controls & PPE (Section 8)**:
    *   **Engineering**: Local exhaust ventilation during cutting.
    *   **Respiratory**: NIOSH-approved N95 particulate respirator.
    *   **Eyes**: Safety glasses with side shields.
    *   **Skin**: Protective gloves and long sleeves.

### B. Joint Compound (Drywall Mud)
*   **Hazards (Section 2)**: Mild eye irritant. Sanding dry compound generates nuisance dust. Long-term exposure to respirable dust can cause lung injury.
*   **Exposure Controls & PPE (Section 8)**:
    *   **PPE**: N95 respirator during sanding; protective safety goggles.

### C. Metal Studs (Galvanized Steel)
*   **Hazards (Section 2)**: Not hazardous in solid form. Cutting, welding, or grinding generates metal dust and fumes containing **iron oxide, zinc oxide, and manganese**.
*   **Exposure Controls & PPE (Section 8)**:
    *   **Engineering**: Mechanical ventilation when cutting or welding.
    *   **PPE**: Cut-resistant leather gloves; protective eyewear; welding respirator if welding.

### D. Carpet Tiles & Adhesives
*   **Hazards (Section 2)**: Adhesives may release Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs). May cause skin sensitization or irritation upon contact.
*   **Exposure Controls & PPE (Section 8)**:
    *   **Engineering**: Ensure dilution ventilation during application.
    *   **PPE**: Nitrile solvent-resistant gloves; safety glasses.
